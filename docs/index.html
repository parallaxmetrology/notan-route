How it is made

The field comes from notan-route, unchanged. tools/export.py reads the picture, then for every 4-pixel cell asks the library for the first stop of a route started there (strength × proximity × connection, no history). A fixed spread of 40 cells is re-checked against the public route() call; the receipt shows how many agree. Inside a mass, onward points to that mass's successor (the field's memoryless next step), so lines run on to the basin ends.

Route so far replays a route (from the centre, or from the strongest mass). At step k its first k stops are taken and damp their neighbourhood exactly as in notan-route's walk; every line then heads for the route's next pick from where it stands, and no line is drawn where the route would end (best score under the floor). Receipt: the same rule, evaluated at each stop's own position, must give the route's actual next stop.

A picture you drop in is read by a JavaScript port of parallax-notan and notan-route (js/notan-route.js), in a worker, in this browser. On the four paintings here it gives the same masses, basins, routes and grid cells as the Python, cell for cell (README: Parity).

Divides: a divide is the boundary between two side-by-side cells whose first stops differ. The picture is re-read after each of notan-route's five changes (2% crop on every side, JPEG quality 60, gamma 0.8 and 1.25, every second pixel); a piece of divide is kept by a change when the changed reading has a divide within the chosen distance (0.01, 0.02 or 0.05 of the frame's long side; one grid cell is about 0.008). Crop readings are mapped back to the full frame. The JPEG uses this browser's encoder, not Pillow's, so that one change can differ from the Python; the other four match it exactly. A kept divide is a divide in the same place: the masses either side may be different ones.

What if changes the picture's own pixels inside the chosen mass, as the reader cut it (its region at the 512 px working size, edge softened by a Gaussian of 1.5 working pixels), and reads the changed picture from the start. Lighter moves each pixel the chosen share of the way to white, darker the chosen share of the way to black, paint out replaces the mass with the mean colour of a ring just outside it. It is a stand-in for repainting, not a model of paint: a change made with a brush will differ.

Compare matches masses between the two readings (same polarity, nearest first, within 0.05 of the frame's long side, one to one). A cell's first stop has changed when B's first stop there is not the match of A's. A basin holds when B has a basin ending within 0.05 of the same place, as in notan-route's stability check. A piece of divide is kept when the other reading has a divide within 0.02.

The lines are drawn by anvaka/streamlines (MIT, vendored unchanged), an implementation of Jobard and Lefer's evenly spaced streamlines. Its default first seed is random; here every drawing is seeded from a fixed lattice, so the same settings give the same lines. Line weight grows along each line towards where it drains.

The lines are a picture of notan-route's rule, not a claim about eyes or viewing order. Where lines from two directions meet head on, the picture is a divide: a step either side leads to a different mass.
